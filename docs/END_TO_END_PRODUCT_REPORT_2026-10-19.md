## New notification and coordination contract

- `GET /v1/meeting-notifications`: eligible viewer’s latest 100 unread, due, unexpired meeting updates only; authoritative meetup membership/status, both accounts’ current access and blocks are checked again. Response contains opaque notification/meeting IDs, type, timestamps and read status. It contains no reason, location, name, message or device token.
- `POST /v1/meeting-notifications/:id/read`: owner-scoped acknowledgement; another account cannot acknowledge or inspect an entry through this route.
- `POST /v1/push-devices`: explicit eligible-user opt-in, validated Expo token, bounded device registration and rate limit. Tokens cannot silently move between accounts. Route returns 409 unless server push is enabled **and** a server-only Expo access token is configured. No token is accepted via a URL or returned by reads.
- `DELETE /v1/push-devices`: authenticated cleanup even after event-access revocation; disables this account’s registered devices. Native logout attempts this cleanup after local logout. An offline device cannot guarantee remote deregistration; notifications contain only a generic instruction to open Meetups, and device registrations expire.
- Meeting creation accepts an optional stable `requestId` bound to its sender and exact payload. Clients retain it across retries in the current composer. Changing a payload creates a new identifier. A lost-response replay returns the same meeting; an identifier reused for another payload is rejected. A partial unique index prevents a second pending invitation to that recipient.
- Acceptance reserves both participants’ 30-minute slot keys in one document using a unique multikey index. Concurrent conflicting accepts cannot both obtain the reservation. A cancelled or erased meeting releases its booking; reconciliation repairs a crash between the status write and cleanup. An unfinished acceptance reservation is retained fail-closed until recovery/retry or expiry, rather than permitting an overlapping booking after a crash.
- Socket.IO is an immediate hint on web; Mongo records are authoritative. Web reloads on reconnect/socket hints and polls status. Native polls while open and refreshes on foreground/notification response. A missing socket/push does not delete a meetup. Native push taps route to Meetups rather than opening an arbitrary supplied URL.

The built-in worker reconciles missed status/notification writes every 30 seconds. Attendee-triggered recovery is scoped to that participant; the background worker uses indexed pending-work flags. Fresh unread push alerts are queued only within an hour of their scheduled time; older updates remain in the in-app inbox until their normal expiry. It stores delivery jobs in Mongo, leases them across workers, backs off on transient failure (six attempts maximum), obtains Expo tickets, checks receipts after 15 minutes and retires `DeviceNotRegistered` tokens. Restarting a worker preserves jobs and ticket IDs. A successful receipt means acceptance by the provider, **not** human receipt or meeting attendance. At-least-once delivery can duplicate an alert after a crash around the network response. Stale reminders are cancelled; pending sends revalidate membership, eligibility, blocks, device ownership and expiry. Already submitted provider messages cannot be recalled.

`PUSH_ENABLED=false` is the default; without explicit enablement and `EXPO_PUSH_ACCESS_TOKEN`, the worker makes no external push calls. Only a fake injected transport was used to test network retry/receipt behavior. Follow the [Expo delivery/receipt guidance](https://docs.expo.dev/push-notifications/sending-notifications/) and [native notification setup](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v58.0.0/sdk/notifications/) when authorization is renewed. Do not enable push just to make a readiness badge pass.

Retention: meeting history is bounded to 30 days for newly created records (latest 50 history rows and nearest 100 upcoming rows per response); meeting notifications 7 days, reminders until start, device registrations 30 days, push jobs at most their alert’s lifetime or a one-day receipt window. Booking expiry is the meeting end. Mongo TTL deletion may lag, so application checks still enforce expiry. Older records with the previous 30-minute TTL were not migrated or recovered. No live index or retention migration was executed.

New collections: `meetingBookings`, `meetingNotifications`, `pushDevices`, `pushDeliveries`. Index definitions are in `src/indexes.js`; populated incompatible indexes fail closed. Before an authorized rollout, inspect pending duplicates and existing reservations/history, back up, review retention with the owner and reconcile the manifest without destructive automatic cleanup. Do not drop populated indexes to make startup succeed.
